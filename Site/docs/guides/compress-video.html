Compress a Video

Drop a video onto a destination card for a one-click result, or open the advanced panel for full control.

The fast way: destination cards

Drag a video directly onto one of the five dock cards:

  • Discord — caps output at 25 MB, 1080p max, 8 Mbps.
  • Email — caps output at 25 MB, 1080p max, 8 Mbps.
  • YouTube — no size cap, 4K (2160p) max, 20 Mbps for upload-quality output.
  • Web / Social — 1080p max, 5 Mbps, no hard size cap.
  • Custom — starts from a balanced 1080p / 5 Mbps baseline that you can override completely in the advanced panel.

Target-size mode

Open the advanced panel and set Target size to a number of megabytes — leave it on its auto placeholder and Dense uses the preset's own bitrate cap instead. When you fill it in, Dense works backward from that ceiling to pick a bitrate that lands under it.

Resolution & format

The Resolution picker offers Preset default, Same as input, 4K (2160p), 1080p, and 720p. The Format picker offers MP4 · H.264, MP4 · HEVC, and MOV (MOV output is encoded with H.264).

Remove audio

Toggle Remove audio in the advanced panel to strip the audio track entirely — useful for silent background loops or screen recordings nobody needs to hear.

Output location, suffix, and cleanup

  • Output: choose Next to original or Custom folder (pick one with the Change… button; it shows None chosen until you do).
  • Suffix: defaults to -compressed, appended to the output filename. Change it to whatever you like.
  • Move to Trash after success: after a confirmation ("Move originals to Trash?"), moves the original to the Trash once compression succeeds. You can always put it back.
Hardware acceleration via VideoToolbox means most 1080p clips compress in a fraction of their own runtime — 4K takes longer but still runs locally.

Batches and cancellation

Drop multiple files or a whole folder to queue them. Each file compresses in isolation, so one bad file doesn't stall the rest. Use Cancel all to stop the whole queue at any time — in-flight jobs stop immediately rather than finishing in the background.

macOS 13+ · Apple Silicon & Intel. Dense uses FFmpeg under the GPL, run as a separate process — build sources: evermeet.cx (x86_64) and osxexperts.net (arm64).
